TournamentIQ – Elo Ratings and Football Intelligence
A data-driven look at team strength during the 2026 FIFA World Cup
1. Introduction
TournamentIQ is an intelligent system that tracks team strength using live 2026 FIFA World Cup data. This article discusses how I used elo ratings to answer one question: who is the strongest team in this tournament, based purely on what has happened on the pitch?
2. What is Elo
To answer that question, this system uses Elo ratings.
Elo is a mathematical system for measuring the strength of a competitor based on their results against other competitors. It was originally designed for chess but maps well to football and is used across several professional sports analytics systems.
The key difference between Elo and a standard league table is what each one measures. A league table tells you what happened. Elo tells you how good a team actually is, accounting for who they beat and by how much. A team can top their group by beating three weak opponents and still have a low Elo. A team that loses narrowly to the tournament favourite might have a higher Elo than their standing suggests.
3. The limitation of Elo from a World Cup Perspective
World Football Elo ratings date back to 1997. Those ratings reflect decades of international results and are useful for ranking teams across world football. But they carry historical points into a tournament that were built long before it started. Historical data cannot answer a different question: which teams have been exceptionally good in this specific tournament, regardless of what came before. TournamentIQ answers that question. Every team starts at the same baseline with no prior history. The ratings are built entirely from what happens in 2026.
4. Calculating Elo Ratings
Expected outcome
Before each match, the system calculates the probability that each team will win. This is called the expected score.
E(A) = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ ((R(B) - R(A)) / 400))






Where:
* E(A) is the expected score for Team A
* R(A) is Team A's current rating
* R(B) is Team B's current rating
* 400 is a scaling constant that controls the steepness of the probability curve


The 400 constant was standardised in chess and inherited by most Elo systems. It controls how steep the probability curve is between two teams. Here is what different rating gaps produce:


* A 400-point gap:
E(A) = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ (-400/400))
     = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ (-1))
     = 1 / (1 + 0.1)
     = 1 / 1.1 = 0.909
The stronger team wins 91% of the time.


* A 200-point gap:
E(A) = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ (-200/400))
     = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ (-0.5))
     = 1 / (1 + 0.316)
     = 1 / 1.316 = 0.760
The stronger team wins 76% of the time.


Equal ratings give exactly 50%. The wider the gap, the more certain the outcome.
New rating
The new Elo rating is calculated after every match based on what actually happened versus what was expected.
New Rating = Old Rating + K x G x (W - E(A))
Where:
* W is the actual result. Win = 1.0, draw = 0.5, loss = 0.0
* Penalty shootout winner: W = 0.75
* Penalty shootout loser: W = 0.5


K is the sensitivity factor — how much a single match can move the rating. It increases in later rounds because higher-stakes matches carry more weight.
* Group stage: K = 32
* Last 32: K = 40
* Round of 16 and Quarterfinals: K = 50
* Semifinals, Final, and 3rd place: K = 60


G is the goal difference multiplier. Bigger wins earn a larger multiplier, so the system rewards dominance rather than just results.
* Win by 0 or 1 goals: G = 1.0
* Win by 2 goals: G = 1.5
* Win by 3 or more goals: G = 1.75
* Penalty shootout: G = 1.0 (penalties are not a measure of dominance)


Zero-sum property: what one team gains, the other loses exactly. Total ratings across all teams stay constant.


5. A worked example: Germany vs Curacao (Group Stage One 2026 World Cup)
Germany beat Curacao 7-1 in the group stage of the 2026 World Cup. Both teams were playing their first match of the tournament, so both started at the baseline rating of 1000. Here is how the new rating is calculated.


Step 1 — Expected score
E(Germany) = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ ((1000 - 1000) / 400))
           = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ 0) = 1 / 2 = 0.5
Because both teams start at 1000, the system treats this as a 50/50 match. It has no prior information about either team.


Step 2 — New rating
K = 32 (group stage). G = 1.75 (winning by 6 goals triggers the 3+ multiplier). W = 1.0 (Germany won in regular time).
New Rating = 1000 + 32 x 1.75 x (1.0 - 0.5)
           = 1000 + 32 x 1.75 x 0.5
           = 1000 + 28 = 1028
Germany ends the match at 1028. Curacao drops to 972, because what Germany gained, Curacao lost exactly.


6. How does this compare to World Football Elo Ratings?
World Football Elo has Germany at 1939 and Curacao at 1436 after this match. TornamentIQ has Germany at 1028 and Curacao at 972. The difference comes from where both teams started before the match.
In World Football Elo, Germany entered with a rating of 1938 and Curacao with 1440, a gap of 498 points. Plug that into the expected score formula:
E(Germany) = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ (-498/400)) = 0.947
Germany was already expected to win 94.7% of the time. So even with a 7-1 result, the surprise factor was tiny.
Surprise factor = W - E(A) = 1.0 - 0.947 = 0.053
According to the World Football ratings awarding system, Germany gained 1 point. Curacao lost 4. The result was almost entirely expected, so the ratings barely moved. In TournamentIQ, both teams started at 1000, so the surprise factor was 0.5, and both teams moved by 28 points. 
This disparity reflects what each system is measuring. World Football Elo brings decades of international data while TournamentIQ starts fresh at the tournament and builds from what happens on the pitch.

8. What is similar and what is different?


Comparing these results to the world football data, all eight quarterfinalists ended up in the top eight. TournamentIQ correctly separated the strong teams from the weak ones across the tournament. The most interesting finding from TournamentIQ is France at number one. While Argentina won the FIFA World Cup 2022, France ranks higher than Argentina in the system. 
Why France ranks higher in TournamentIQ
France scored 16 goals across 7 matches. The goal multiplier applied at 1.75 in two matches and 1.5 in three others, meaning the system rewarded them for winning by a margin, not just winning. That is dominance. Their only loss came against Tunisia in the group stage, which cost them 18.7 points. From the Round of 16 onward, they did not lose a single match in regular time. Their biggest single gain came in the semifinal against Morocco, where a 2-0 win with K = 60 added 42 points in one match. In the Final, they came back from 2-0 down to draw 3-3, and despite losing on penalties, the system gave them W = 0.75 rather than 0, adding another 14.2 points. They ended the tournament at 1142.

Why are FIFA World Cup 2026 results different from TournamentIQ? 
Using Elo Ratings history, Argentina arrived at the 2022 World Cup with 2143 points while France arrived with 2004 points, a gap of 139 points before a single match was played. France produced more dominant score lines across the tournament and earned higher goal multipliers, but they were closing a gap built up over years of international football, not just these seven matches.
Two teams in the comparison table make this point clearly. Belgium appears in the World Football top 10 but not in TournamentIQ. They carried a strong pre-tournament rating built over years of results but went out in the group stage. TournamentIQ saw none of that history so ranked them accordingly. Japan is the opposite. They appear in my top 10 but not in World Football's. They beat Germany and Spain in the group stage, two of the biggest upsets of the tournament. TournamentIQ rewarded that fully because it had no prior history pulling their rating down.




Conclusion
The two systems are answering different questions. World Football Elo measures who is the stronger team in world football, including everything they have done before this tournament. TournamentIQ measures who was the stronger team in Qatar 2022, regardless of what came before. In most cases the answers overlap. France and Argentina are the top three in both. But at the edges, Belgium dropping out and Japan appearing, the gap between historical weight and tournament performance becomes visible.
9. Using TournamentIQ Elo Ratings for FIFA World Cup 2026
Volatility after the first fixture - Group Stage One
Elo ratings answer “who is strongest,” and they can also be used to answer which groups have already started to separate, and which are still wide open. Using Elo ratings to calculate standard deviation tells us how spread out the four teams in a group are after their opening match.
A higher standard deviation means the group has already split, one or two teams clearly ahead. A lower standard deviation means the four teams are still bunched together, nothing decided yet. All teams started with a baseline rating of 1000.
A Worked Example: Group J
After the first fixture, Group J stood at Argentina 1028, Austria 1024, Jordan 976, Algeria 972.
Step 1, mean
Mean = (1028 + 1024 + 976 + 972) / 4 = 1000
Step 2, standard deviation
Each team's deviation from the mean:
* Algeria: 972 − 1000 = −28
Standard deviation = √[ Σ(x − mean)² / (n − 1) ]
= √[ (28² + 24² + (−24)² + (−28)²) / 3 ]
= √[ (784 + 576 + 576 + 784) / 3 ]
= √[ 2720 / 3 ]
= √906.67 = 30.11

Validating the results
To validate that this formula was not hallucinating, I ran the same calculation on the 2022 World Cup, using only each team's opening match. Group A came out highest by a wide margin. Group A was the group with Argentina's shock loss to Saudi Arabia, the biggest upset of that tournament, and it ended with the widest points spread of any group at the World Cup. I also checked the underlying results against FIFA's official standings page. Every team showing 0 standard deviation had, in fact, drawn their opener and sat on 1 point, confirming that the database matched the official record.


10. What comes next
Elo in this system ranks team strength and produces a win probability for a given matchup; it does not predict a scoreline. The expected score formula returns a single number between 0 and 1, the probability one team beats the other. Nothing in the formula outputs a number of goals. Predicting an actual score is a separate problem, planned for the Poisson probability model in the next phase of this project, which will use goals scored, goals conceded, and Elo win probability as inputs to estimate a distribution of likely scores for each team, rather than a single number.
I will be comparing my live 2026 ratings against World Football Elo as the tournament progresses. Where they agree, the signal is strong. Where they diverge, that gap is usually the most interesting story: a team outperforming their historical reputation or a heavyweight performing below their usual level.
